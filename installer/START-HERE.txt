PEARL PULL - PARENT SETUP

1. Install PearlPull-Receiver-Setup.exe on PC 2 (the trapdoor PC).
2. Install PearlPull-Button-Setup.exe on PC 1 (the player/Neo PC).
   Sign in as the child who plays Minecraft when installing each app.
3. Close the old PowerShell script windows. Existing pairing is reused.
4. On PC 2, open Receiver. Click Allow home network (parent) and approve
   Windows' prompt. The home network must be marked Private.
5. Update BOTH apps to version 0.6.0 or newer for approval pairing.
   On PC 1, open Button. If not already paired, enter PC 2's home IPv4
   address and click Pair with PC 2. Compare the short codes on both screens,
   then click Accept pairing on PC 2 within one minute. Nothing to copy!
   Only accept a matching request you started. Check connection sends no click.
   Existing pairing and Neo buttons keep working after upgrades.
6. Click Copy Neo button URL. In Stream Deck, use System > Website,
   paste the URL, and enable GET request in background.
   Open button artwork to find the included pearl icon.
7. On PC 2, bring Minecraft to the front and aim at the trapdoor.
   Practice one pearl pull. Re-arm the chamber after use.

DAILY USE

Start Minecraft. Pearl Pull starts at Windows sign-in and runs in the tray.
Press the Neo button or F8 on PC 1. Closing the settings window keeps it running.
Double-click the pearl near the Windows clock to check status. Windows may
place it under the tray's up-arrow. Right-click > Quit stops it deliberately.
Keep PC 2 awake and signed in. The app does not aim or re-arm the chamber.

TROUBLESHOOTING

Open either app and click Open logs. Logs contain matching request IDs and are
limited to about 10 MiB per role (five sessions, 1 MiB plus one backup each).
The app checks the connection every 30 seconds and retries failed listeners.
No old click is saved to fire when a disconnected PC comes back.
Startup uses Task Scheduler with crash recovery where allowed; restricted
accounts fall back to ordinary sign-in startup. Check startup.configured or
startup.task_unavailable in the logs to see which method is active.

These initial installers and apps are unsigned. If Smart App Control blocks
them, installation does not bypass it. A trusted signed release is required
for that deployment. Do not turn off protections merely to test the installer.
